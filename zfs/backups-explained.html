Five layers between your files and losing them

The mirror keeps working when a disk dies. It does nothing when you delete a file, because the delete lands on both disks at once. So there are more layers: snapshots to undo mistakes, a nightly copy to a second pool, health checks to catch dying disks, and later an off-site copy for fire or theft.

Checkpoint 11
How far back you can go · Documents, Photos, Nextcloud, Apps

Lots of recent snapshots, fewer old ones

Each mark is one snapshot kept. Every row has its own time scale. Newer snapshots are close together, so you can undo this afternoon to the hour. Older ones thin out, so you can still go back a year. This is the classic grandfather-father-son rotation. The oldest of each kind is pruned when a new one is taken.

What a snapshot costs

Nothing when taken. It only keeps blocks that change afterwards. Photos and documents are mostly added to, rarely edited, so snapshots stay tiny. Today's 22 snapshots use 128 KB.

The catch: deleted space comes back late

Delete 50 GB of photos and the space returns only when the last snapshot holding them expires, up to 12 months later. To free it sooner, delete those snapshots by hand.

Why not keep everything forever?

Unlimited snapshots grow without bound and make the list unusable. CISA asks for at least 7 days of rollback. A year covers "I noticed months later".

Every night at 03:00

The nightly copy to tank500gb

syncoid --no-sync-snap --create-bookmark tank1tb/Documents tank500gb/Documents
The whole job, once per dataset, in /usr/local/sbin/syncoid-backup-tank1tb-to-tank500gb.
--no-sync-snap
Send only sanoid's snapshots, no extra ones of its own. The copy ends up with the same hourly/daily/weekly/monthly history.
--create-bookmark
Leave a tiny marker on the source. Even if sanoid has pruned the snapshot sent last night, tomorrow's copy can still be incremental.
readonly=on
Set on every copy, so nothing edits tank500gb by accident. You can still browse and copy out.
OnCalendar=03:00 · Persistent=true
If MOTHERLODE was off at 03:00, the copy runs right after it boots.

Space: tank500gb holds about 450 GB, tank1tb about 899 GB. When the five copied datasets near 400 GB together, we drop one from the list.

Why timers, not cron

systemd timers are the standard on current Debian, and Debian's sanoid package already ships one. A timer runs a missed job after boot, never starts a second copy while one is still running, logs to the journal, and every schedule shows in one command.

The cron file you might spot

/etc/cron.d/sanoid comes with the package. It only runs on machines without systemd, so it never fires here.

Decision for Checkpoint 8

One Photos folder, not two

Same idea for Documents. To build in Checkpoint 8: NEXTCLOUD_MOUNT=/tank1tb so the containers can see the folders, and default ACLs giving both group nas and Nextcloud's user (uid 33) write access, so phone uploads stay editable from Windows. No backup change needed.

Found during testing

The Toshiba can't finish a surface test

What happened

The long SMART test stopped within minutes: Completed: read failure at LBA 1904285592, 908 GiB into the 931.5 GiB disk, inside the ZFS partition. It is the same sector that has failed every test since hour 22,058. 24 sectors are waiting to be remapped, 0 remapped so far.

Is data at risk?

Not today. Nothing is stored there yet, the WD holds a full mirror copy, and tank500gb has a third copy every night. But it is the weakest disk in the pool that matters most.

What to do

Recommended: replace it with any new ≥1 TB disk. One zpool replace, and the pool stays online while it copies from the WD. Writing over the bad sectors to force a remap is possible, but it is a disk-level write and needs your explicit go-ahead.
